# Positioning & SEO Strategy — Theirs (`theirs.page`)

> **"It should feel like visiting someone’s life, not visiting their obituary."**  
> But to be found, trusted, and chosen by families, our public category must be crystal clear.

---

## 1. The Category: Online Memorial Website

Your category is **not** “life archive.”  
It is **not** “digital legacy.”  
It is **not** “a place on the internet dedicated to a human life.”

Your category is:
# **Online memorial website**

This is the exact phrase that both users and search engines immediately recognize and understand.

### Why Category Clarity Matters
Current competitors repeatedly anchor themselves around *online memorial*, *memorial website*, *memorial page*, and *loved one*:
- **ForeverMissed** leads with *"Create a Memorial Website"*
- **CreateMemorial** leads with *"Free Online Memorial Website"*
- **Remembr** describes *"memorial pages"*
- **Keeper** uses *"Online Memorials"*

Digital memorial search demand is a focused category: a 2026 Google Ads / Keyword Planner study estimated the broader US digital/QR memorial cluster at approximately **5,610 searches/month**. Invented terminology like "life archive" or "collaborative remembrance infrastructure" dilutes search indexing and leaves intent ambiguous.

## 2. Locked Positioning Statement

> **Theirs is an online memorial website where families create a memorial for someone they love, bring together their photos, audios and videos, stories and tributes, and invite family and friends to contribute their own memories.**

### What It Is NOT (Publicly)
- ❌ **life archive**
- ❌ **memory preservation platform**
- ❌ **collaborative remembrance infrastructure**

*These phrases may describe the technical or emotional soul of the product internally, but they must NOT define the category publicly.*

## 3. Site-Wide Vocabulary Rules

All developers, designers, copywriters, and AI agents must use these terms consistently across UI copy, metadata, schema markup, headlines, button labels, transactional emails, and marketing:

| Dimension | Standard Terminology |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Category** | `Online memorial` / `Online memorial website` |
| **Product Object** | `Memorial` / `Memorial website` / `Memorial page` |
| **Primary Action** | `Create a memorial` |
| **Audience & Subject** | `Loved one` / `Family and friends` |
| **Content Types** | `Photos, stories, memories, tributes, voice and video` |
| **Collaboration** | `Invite family and friends to contribute` |
| **Paid Outcome** | `Complete memorial` / `Family archive` |

### Terms to Avoid Leading With
Do not lead with these terms in headlines, titles, meta tags, or primary marketing copy:
- `life archive`
- `digital legacy`
- `legacy platform`
- `memory platform`
- `virtual memorial`
- `digital remembrance space`

*Note: They can appear naturally in deep supporting prose, help articles, or SEO guides, but they must never compete with "online memorial website" for homepage and brand category meaning.*

## 5. Strategic SEO Decision: Memorial vs. Obituary

### Search Demand Reality (2026 Data)
- **Obituary Search Volume**: ~616,900 monthly US searches
- **Digital/QR Memorial Cluster**: ~5,610 monthly US searches

### The Decision: Do NOT Chase "Obituary" on the Homepage
Chasing "obituary" in the homepage title or primary H1 just because of larger search volume would attract the wrong user intent (e.g., people looking for funeral notices, death records, newspaper print notices, or service times). Distorting the homepage damages conversion and confuses search rankings.

### Dedicated Content Strategy
Instead of distorting the homepage, capture high-intent obituary search demand through dedicated editorial content and comparison pages:
1. `Online Obituary vs Online Memorial` — Explaining the difference between a static death notice and an alive, collaborative memorial website.
2. `How to Write an Obituary for a Loved One` — Compassionate step-by-step guide that introduces Theirs as the permanent home for memories.
3. `Create an Online Obituary and Memorial` — Dual-intent landing page for people tasked with both requirements.

These dedicated pages capture obituary search demand and seamlessly introduce Theirs without compromising the homepage's category signal.
